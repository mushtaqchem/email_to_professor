# Professor Email Templates for MS & PhD Scholarships

Free, editable Microsoft Word email templates to help students contact professors about MS and PhD opportunities, research positions, academic supervision, and scholarships abroad.

This repository provides four practical email templates for different situations, helping students write professional, relevant, and effective emails to prospective supervisors.

## 📥 Download the Templates

Download the Word files directly from this repository:

- **Template 1:** Personalized Cold Email Based on Research Papers
- **Template 2:** Simplified Cold Email Based on Research Background
- **Template 3:** Emailing a Professor About an Advertised Position
- **Template 4:** Follow-up Email When a Professor Does Not Respond

Open the `.docx` files in Microsoft Word or a compatible word processor and customize them according to your academic background and research interests.

## 📚 What's Included?

### 1. Personalized Cold Email Based on Research Papers

This approach involves reviewing a professor's relevant research papers and identifying specific connections between their work and your academic interests.

**Best for:** Students who want to demonstrate a deeper understanding of a professor's research and write a highly personalized email.

**Advantages:**
- Demonstrates a specific interest in the professor's work.
- Helps establish a clear connection between your research interests and theirs.
- May lead to better results when the email demonstrates a genuine research fit.

**Limitation:** Requires more time and effort because you need to read relevant research papers.

### 2. Simplified Cold Email Based on Research Background

This approach requires less preparation. Instead of reading research papers in detail, you focus on understanding the professor's research background, main interests, and research group.

**Best for:** Students who want to contact relevant professors more efficiently.

**Advantages:**
- Requires less time and preparation.
- Makes it easier to contact multiple professors.
- Still allows you to personalize your email based on research interests.

**Limitation:** The email may lack the specific research insights that can be demonstrated through Template 1.

### 3. Emailing a Professor About an Advertised Position

Use this template when a professor has announced an MS or PhD position, research opportunity, or funded vacancy.

**Tips:**
- Clearly mention the advertised position.
- Explain how your academic background matches its requirements.
- Highlight relevant research experience and skills.
- Follow the application instructions provided in the announcement.

### 4. Follow-up Email When a Professor Does Not Respond

Use this template when you have contacted a professor but have not received a response.

**Tips:**
- Allow a reasonable amount of time before following up.
- Keep the email short and polite.
- Refer to your previous email.
- Avoid sending repeated reminders within a short period.

## 🎯 Which Template Should You Use?

| Situation | Recommended template |
|---|---|
| You can invest time in detailed research | Template 1 |
| You prefer a quicker, less demanding approach | Template 2 |
| You are contacting a professor about an advertised position | Template 3 |
| You have already emailed but received no response | Template 4 |

**My recommendation:** Template 1 is worth the extra effort when you want to demonstrate a strong, specific connection with a professor's research. Template 2 is a practical alternative when you need to save time while still personalizing your emails.

Neither approach guarantees a response, admission, supervision, an acceptance letter, or a scholarship. Your academic profile, research fit, email quality, funding availability, and timing also matter.

## ✍️ How to Use the Templates

1. Download the `.docx` file from this repository.
2. Open it in Microsoft Word or a compatible word processor.
3. Custamize it with your own information.
4. Customize the email for the specific professor and opportunity.
5. Verify the professor's research interests and application requirements.
6. Proofread your email before sending it.

### Important Tips

- Use a clear and professional subject line.
- Provide accurate information about your education and research experience.
- Explain why you are interested in the professor's work.
- Keep your email concise and relevant.
- Attach your CV and other documents when appropriate.
- Avoid sending identical, generic emails to multiple professors.
